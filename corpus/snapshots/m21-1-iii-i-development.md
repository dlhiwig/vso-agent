## Who obtains evidence (III.i.2.A)

Claimants are ultimately responsible for providing evidence. VA must still request what it needs from records already available, develop additional evidence the claimant or representative identifies, make reasonable efforts for Federal records, and make reasonable efforts for private records the claimant identifies and authorizes. VA has no duty to assist when there is no reasonable possibility that assistance would substantiate the claim.

## Section 5103 notice (III.i.2.B)

VA must tell the claimant what information and medical or lay evidence is still needed to substantiate the claim. Notice may be delivered by more than a paper letter. There are exceptions, including some claims received within one year of a prior sufficient 5103 notice.

## Federal vs private records (III.i.2.C)

A substantially complete initial or supplemental claim triggers reasonable efforts to obtain relevant Federal records: STRs, other service-department records (personnel, LOD, inpatient, behavioral health not in the STR packet), VA and Vet Center records, SSA, Public Health Service, Labor, and other Federal custodians. Reasonable efforts on Federal records continue until the records arrive or it is reasonably certain they do not exist or further effort would be futile.

Private / non-Federal records: VA assists when the claimant adequately identifies the source and provides authorization.

## Relevance (III.i.2.E)

Relevant records relate to the disability or injury claimed and have a reasonable possibility of helping substantiate the claim. Golz v. Shinseki, 590 F.3d 1317 (Fed. Cir. 2010): not every medical record is relevant. Case-by-case. Clinical mental health records are generally treated as relevant when mental health is at issue.

The agent lists what is `have` / `requested` / `va-to-obtain`. It does not send 5103 letters or VBMS record requests.
