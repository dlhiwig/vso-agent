# M21-1 excerpt — lay evidence

Official KnowVA (article location as indexed 2026): https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000014389/M21-1,-Part-V,-Subpart-ii,-Chapter-1,-Section-B---Determining-Disability,-Unemployability,-and-Marginal-Employment

Lay evidence is acceptable to help establish service incurrence or aggravation. For a combat Veteran or former prisoner of war, satisfactory lay evidence consistent with the circumstances, conditions, or hardships of that service can prevail even without official records of the incurrence — but medical evidence of a link to a current condition is still needed to establish service connection.

A non-combat Veteran's lay statements may also be acceptable for incurrence or aggravation and must be weighed against other evidence, including the absence of supporting military records.

A claimant's own statement covering symptoms the claimant can actually describe may constitute evidence to that extent. A medically untrained person is not usually competent to offer a medical etiology opinion; that kind of statement generally carries little weight.

The packet assembler may index a lay / buddy statement as have / requested / missing. It must not draft a false witness statement.
