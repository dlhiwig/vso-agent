# 38 CFR 3.159 Department of Veterans Affairs assistance in developing claims (excerpt)

Official: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-38/section-3.159

Snapshot date: 2026-09-19
Note: Excerpt of definitions and the duty-to-assist frame. The full section is longer.

## (a) Definitions (selected)

Competent medical evidence means evidence provided by a person who is qualified through education, training, or experience to offer medical diagnoses, statements, or opinions. Competent medical evidence may also mean statements conveying sound medical principles found in medical treatises. It would also include statements contained in authoritative writings such as medical and scientific articles and research reports or analyses.

Competent lay evidence means any evidence not requiring that the proponent have specialized education, training, or experience. Lay evidence is competent if it is provided by a person who has knowledge of facts or circumstances and conveys matters that can be observed and described by a lay person.

## Duty to assist (frame)

VA must make reasonable efforts to help the claimant obtain evidence necessary to substantiate the claim, including relevant Federal records and, when the regulatory elements are met, a medical examination or opinion. For private records VA generally needs the claimant's authorization. An exam is not automatic: there must be competent evidence of current disability or persistent symptoms, an in-service event or disease or a service-connected disability, and an indication the two may be associated.

The agent does not request VA exams. The accredited VSO decides what to ask VA to develop.
