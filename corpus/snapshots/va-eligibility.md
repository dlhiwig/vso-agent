# VA: Eligibility for disability benefits

Official: https://www.va.gov/disability/eligibility/
Publisher: U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs
Snapshot date: 2026-09-19

VA disability compensation provides tax-free monthly payments. If you have a service-connected condition, you may be eligible for compensation. A service-connected condition means an illness or injury that was caused by—or got worse because of—your active military service.

You may be eligible for VA disability benefits or compensation if you meet both of these requirements.

Both of these must be true:

- You have a current illness or injury (known as a condition) that affects your mind or body, and
- You served on active duty, active duty for training, or inactive duty training

And at least one of these must be true:

- You got sick or injured while serving in the military—and can link this condition to your illness or injury (called an in-service disability claim), or
- You had an illness or injury before you joined the military—and serving made it worse (called a pre-service disability claim), or
- You have a disability related to your active-duty service that didn’t appear until after you ended your service (called a post-service disability claim)

Presumptive conditions: for some conditions, VA automatically assumes (or “presumes”) that your service caused your condition. If you have a presumptive condition, you don’t need to prove that your service caused the condition. You only need to meet the service requirements for the presumption.

Examples of covered conditions listed by VA include chronic back pain resulting in a current diagnosed back disability, breathing problems from a current lung condition, severe hearing loss, scar tissue, and loss of range of motion.

If you received an other than honorable, bad conduct, or dishonorable discharge, you may not be eligible. Options include a discharge upgrade or a VA Character of Discharge review.
