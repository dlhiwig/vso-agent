# M21-1 III.i.2.E — Relevance determinations (excerpt)

Official: https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000046269/M21-1-Part-III-Subpart-i-Chapter-2-Section-E-Relevance-Determinations
Change date noted on KnowVA: December 5, 2024 (page also marked updated May 27, 2025)
Snapshot date: 2026-09-19
Pair with 38 U.S.C. 5103A; Golz v. Shinseki, 590 F.3d 1317 (Fed. Cir. 2010).

Relevant records, for duty-to-assist purposes, are records that relate to the disability or injury for which the claimant is seeking benefits and that have a reasonable possibility of helping to substantiate the claim.

Golz: not every medical record has a reasonable possibility of helping; the duty applies only to relevant records. The same relevance idea applies to private and Federal records.

Raugust v. Shinseki, 23 Vet. App. 475 (2010): VA has no duty to obtain records without a specific reason to believe the identified records contain information needed to substantiate the claim.

Clinical mental health records are treated as relevant when the claim puts mental health in issue — confirm the live article before relying on that sentence.

A VSO should still identify why a private or SSA file might matter. "Get everything" is not the legal test; "this file may show treatment for the claimed condition" is.
